Speech2Avatar — Speech-Driven 3D Talking-Head Generation

Machine Intelligence Lab · Ongoing research · Mar 2026 – present

Ongoing Research

How should speech be mapped into facial geometry so that expression and identity survive the rendering step — from parameterized expressions into Gaussian-splatted appearance?

Research direction. Working along the audio → facial-representation → rendering chain: person-specific audio-to-expression models, FLAME/3DMM and Pixel3DMM-based facial tracking, and Gaussian-Splatting-based talking-head rendering. The open question is where expression information is best represented — in a parametric face, in a learned latent, or in the splat field itself.

Systems component. The same line carries a deployment track on NVIDIA Jetson AGX Orin (64 GB, JetPack 6.2): Jetson-native installation of the speech and avatar stack, a latency-attribution benchmark for 2-second chunks, and comparison of a teacher renderer against distilled fixed-identity student variants. Measured renderer-level speedups of roughly 20–28× for student variants; the end-to-end pipeline remained short of real-time, with the remaining bottleneck in the upstream batch-1 speech decode rather than the renderer. Quantization experiments (4-bit / Triton-INT4 kernels) and a benchmarked power-mode correction fed this diagnosis; TensorRT export is the documented next step.

  • Methods3D Gaussian Splatting · FLAME · Pixel3DMM · PyTorch · CUDA · Jetson AGX Orin
  • EstablishedPerson-specific tracking and animation pipelines; multi-GPU inference harnesses; Jetson benchmarks and bottleneck attribution
  • Still openReal-time end-to-end inference; final engine qualification; external evaluation; a paper is not yet claimed

PNEUMA — Perceptual Noesis for Episodic Universal Memory Architecture

Undergraduate thesis · North South University · defended Dec 2025 · supervised by Dr. Nabeel Mohammed

Thesis

Can an agent ask "where did I leave my keys?" and get a temporally grounded answer from years of egocentric video — on consumer hardware?

PNEUMA is a model-agnostic long-term video memory system. Video is segmented into chunks with attention-based frame selection; each chunk is described by aspect-specific embeddings (action, object, spatial, audio, visual) and stored across three complementary tiers — structured temporal and action attributes (SQL), week-indexed semantic search (FAISS), and temporal-sequence relations (knowledge graphs). An adaptive cost-based query planner selects a retrieval path per query, so natural-language questions over long archives stay answerable on consumer hardware, without a cloud dependency.

  • MethodsSQL · FAISS · Knowledge Graphs · Vision-Language Models · PyTorch
  • StatusCompleted capstone thesis — manuscript unpublished; larger-scale evaluations remain future work

Ongoing research directions

Earlier-stage work, listed to show current direction rather than as results.

  • Predictive representation learning for long video

    Exploring JEPA-style predictive objectives for video representations, combined with hierarchical memory and structured retrieval — directed at the same long-horizon goal as PNEUMA: memory-driven video understanding that stays tractable on modest hardware. Exploratory; no results claimed yet.

  • HiberMem — evidence-aware agent memory

    Studies what agents should keep in memory: whether annotating which past answers were actually supported by retained evidence improves the selection of retention policies beyond directly measuring task utility. Early gated experiments only; the full system is deferred until those results qualify.

Real-Time Talking Avatars for Robotic Faces

RobotBulls × NSU Machine Intelligence Lab · 2025

Industry Research

Interactive streaming avatars need lip sync across many chunks of audio, aligned to a reference video — but the standard inference path assumes a single GPU and a single pass over the whole input.

My contribution. I developed a distributed multi-GPU inference pipeline for a MuseTalk-based talking-avatar system: audio processing runs on a dedicated GPU instance while generation is spread across several GPU instances (tested up to five), and the final frames are assembled from the distributed outputs behind a streaming HTTP interface. The pipeline kept end-to-end response times under 4 seconds, with silence handling and interruption behavior implemented. Distributed testing proved the hard part — output quality became dependent on network conditions outside the pipeline's control.

  • MethodsMuseTalk · Multi-GPU Inference · Chunked Execution · Streaming HTTP · RunPod
  • StatusOperational pipeline; silence blending and distributed-environment robustness remained open at handoff

Audio2Face-Driven 3D Avatar & Blendshape Recovery

RobotBulls × NSU Machine Intelligence Lab · 2025

Industry Research

A real-time 3D avatar with natural facial motion — and turning the vertex output of an audio-driven model into a form animators can actually use.

My contribution. I integrated an NVIDIA Audio2Face-trained model with a Three.js visualization pipeline, and investigated recovering ARKit-style blendshape weights from raw vertex output via linear optimization — reaching roughly 90% agreement with NVIDIA's microservice on the tested model. The practical limits were 3D model format and texturing (glb/usd), heavy computation for long sequences, and per-model retuning.

  • MethodsNVIDIA Audio2Face · Three.js · ARKit Blend Shapes · Linear Least Squares
  • StatusFunctioning prototype; blendshape computation and emotion specification remained open

Other Program Workstreams

RobotBulls × NSU Machine Intelligence Lab · 2025 — lab-team work across the program

Industry Research

About

Research trajectory

My path into research started with problem solving. Competitive programming taught me to take systems apart and reason about their components; coursework in deep learning and pattern recognition turned that habit toward models, beginning with knowledge distillation for unsupervised object localization and, soon after, a project that combined retrieval-augmented generation with large language models.

Joining the Machine Intelligence Lab in 2025 moved me from reading papers to building systems under real constraints: real-time talking heads, distributed GPU inference, and the latency and memory limits of actual hardware. Building PNEUMA for long egocentric video turned a practical question into a research one — what should a computational memory keep, structure, and forget — and left me skeptical that autoregressive transformers are the right substrate for every kind of intelligence.

That skepticism points toward predictive representation learning and world models, JEPA-style learning in particular, and toward memory systems that behave less like databases and more like cognition. The common thread is intelligent systems that perceive, remember, and act within realistic computational budgets. I intend to keep working on these problems in graduate research, and in the long term to build a career in academia.
