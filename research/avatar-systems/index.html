Applied research & systems

Speech-Driven Avatar Systems

Connecting conversational speech models to visual renderers, with studies of streaming, representation bridges, and deployment limits.

MILab and industry collaboration2025–presentSpeech, vision, and efficient inference

Collaborative applied research. Portions are closed source. The architecture and measurements below retain the distinction between individual, team, and upstream contributions.

From speech to a conversational avatar

The Speech2Avatar work began with conversational lip-sync pipelines that had to preserve chronological frame delivery, synchronize browser audio and video, and recover cleanly from interruption. Later systems replaced text or audio-file boundaries with hidden-state bridges from Moshi/PersonaPlex to IMTalker and Gaussian-based renderers.

The technical focus then shifted from “can these models be connected?” to “which stage limits the system under a defined latency budget?” That produced adapter training, renderer export and validation, fixed-identity distillation, CUDA-event profiling, and a negative-result 3DGS research branch.

From conversational orchestration to representation bridges

Conversational pipeline

Threaded orchestration, bounded queues, sequence reordering, audio-anchored frame scheduling, browser playback, silence handling, and interruption-safe stale-job rejection.

Representation bridge

A 12.5 Hz, 4096-dimensional speech-model state is mapped into Wav2Vec-style conditioning for the visual generator, with an explicit 0.96-second emission contract.

Adapter training

The project archive records 6,890 synchronized examples and distributed adapter training; RMS-weighted fine-tuning reduced silent-period motion by about 17% while reported speech behavior changed by about 2%.

Streaming semantics

Generation counters, queue purges, state carry, binary A/V messages, and timestamped playback keep interruption and delivery behavior separate from model quality claims.

Evaluation boundary

The RobotBulls branch contains substantial orchestration and a silence-lip metric family, but no repository-preserved FPS, latency, VRAM, or metric outputs. This dossier does not attach invented performance values to that branch.

Renderer acceleration and end-to-end limits

On Jetson AGX Orin, TensorRT export reduced the isolated IMTalker renderer but still produced roughly 9 frames per second. A later fixed-identity student study measured 19.84× and 27.62× renderer-stage speedups in the preserved 30 W comparison. The full pipeline remained above its real-time factor target: RTF 4.46 and 4.08 for the two students.

The useful result was attribution. Renderer share fell from 74% to 9.3%, while PersonaPlex accounted for 85.9% of the Student-2 service. Accelerating the renderer therefore changed the next engineering decision instead of proving an end-to-end deployment claim.

Contributions and external components

External / frozen foundation

Moshi and PersonaPlex, IMTalker, MuseTalk, F5-TTS, Wav2Vec2, GaussianTalker, and the public datasets and renderer checkpoints.

Documented custom project work

Representation adapters, synchronized paired data, streaming state carry, A/V transport, TensorRT export rewrites and validation, profiling, student renderers, and experiment governance.

Explicitly attributable to Tanvir

Distributed multi-GPU inference pipelines and the NVIDIA Audio2Face–Three.js / ARKit blendshape investigation are supported by the CV. Deeper repository modules are described as collaborative where file-level attribution is unresolved.

Inherited, planned, or incomplete

Parts of the live PersonaPlex–IMTalker server and several training artifacts were inherited or collaborative. Full-scale student training, release qualification, and deployed INT4 execution were not completed in the audited archive.

The 3D Gaussian branch produced a negative method result

A separate StreamTalker-GS line investigated whether affine covariance transport in FLAME-bound Gaussian avatars produced a residual rendering benefit. Projected footprint conditions were measurable, but the preregistered residual-association criterion failed for all three tested identities and the causal swap was not executed. The improvement claim was frozen.
