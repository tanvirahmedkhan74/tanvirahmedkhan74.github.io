RSI — Capacity Growth in Verified Self-Improvement

Can controlled capacity growth restore learning after a language model reaches a verified self-training plateau?

October 2026–presentLanguage model self-improvementEngineering pilotUpdated 13 October 2026

Current status: ongoing engineering and recovery validation. The main growth hypothesis has not yet been tested.

Research overview

RSI studies recursive verified self-improvement: a language model proposes tasks and trains on responses accepted by an objective verifier. The research question is whether function-preserving expansion can restore held-out learning once a functioning self-training process reaches a qualified plateau.

The intended comparison separates capacity growth from continued adaptation and other training effects. Model expansion is a hypothesis to evaluate, rather than a demonstrated source of improvement.

Engineering pilot

The prototype implements task proposal, verification, accepted-response training, evaluation, compute accounting, and complete experiment-state recovery. Initial tests support bounded supervised-training feasibility and consistency across saved-state restarts.

The autonomous pilot did not produce usable training tasks, and a skipped training update prevented the recovery acceptance criterion from passing. A failed task generator is not evidence of a learning plateau.

Research contribution and current work

My work includes the verified-training pipeline, recovery infrastructure, experimental accounting, and controlled study design. It uses established pretrained models and training frameworks.

Current priorities are reliable verified-target production and fresh-session recovery. Architectural growth, recursive learning benefits, and held-out generalization remain untested. The project has no submitted or published paper.
