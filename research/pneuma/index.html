PNEUMA — Video memory for long-form question answering

How can one moment in a long video remain accessible through several retrieval paths?

Completed undergraduate thesis · Four-person team · December 2025

On this page

  • What we built
  • What happened
  • What it led to

What we built

PNEUMA was our four-person undergraduate thesis at North South University, supervised by Dr. Nabeel Mohammed and completed in December 2025. I designed the methodology and the pipeline and led the work; four of us built it.

A person eating an apple can be recalled as an action, an object, a place, a sound, or a neighbouring event. We wanted one moment in a long video to stay reachable through each of these paths instead of a single flattened embedding.

The system ran pretrained perception and language models over a video and stored their descriptions in three places: SQL records, vector indices for separate aspects, and a temporal graph. A query was answered by combining structured lookup, vector retrieval and temporal relations. No model was trained; this was an integration and memory system built around upstream models.

What happened

The architecture was ambitious. When we evaluated it, we found unresolved problems in grounding, consistency and correctness, and I could not trace how the draft scores had been produced. I do not report those numbers. We did not continue the direction after the thesis.

What it led to

The experience changed what I look for. I became less interested in whether a system can show an apparently intelligent behaviour, and more interested in which components and representations actually make that behaviour possible.

Which stored item supplied the evidence? Which one only helped us reach it? Which apparent capability came from an upstream model? These are the questions I now study in ERL.
