StreamTalker-GS — Speech-Driven 3DGS Experiments

An experimental investigation of affine covariance transport in FLAME-driven Gaussian avatars.

Testing a geometric hypothesis

This work investigated whether affine covariance transport could improve FLAME-driven Gaussian avatar rendering. The motivation was geometric: a local deformation can change a Gaussian’s shape in ways that a similarity transform cannot express. For that distinction to help rendering, however, it also needed to relate to the image residual we were trying to reduce.

This investigation grew out of our collaborative speech-avatar systems work. It built on Moshi/Mimi, GaussianTalker, FLAME 2020, Pixel3DMM, HuBERT, and existing Gaussian rasterization methods. The contribution here was the covariance-transport question and its experimental interpretation; the broader streaming and conditioning work belongs to the collaboration.

What the experiments showed

Across three identities and approximately 1.04 million supported observations, affine and similarity transport produced measurable differences in projected covariance. Those differences did not consistently track the tested rendering residual.

Separate analytic-to-rasterizer controls agreed to a maximum radial RMS difference of 5.02×10−7 px and responded monotonically to filter and overlap changes. These checks gave confidence in the geometric measurements, but did not demonstrate an improvement to avatar rendering.

Why the hypothesis was set aside

The geometric difference alone was insufficient motivation for the next intervention. The causal H4 renderer swap and the broader supervision-transfer program were not completed, and the experiments established no rendering improvement. The covariance-transport hypothesis was therefore set aside.

The useful lesson was to test the link between a plausible geometric mechanism and the error it is meant to explain before investing in a larger training program. This is a completed experimental investigation with a negative finding.
