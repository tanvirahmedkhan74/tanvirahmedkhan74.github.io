RIFT — Recoverability-Gated Implicit Facial Dynamics

How much facial motion does speech determine, and how does that predictability relate to visible articulation?

September 2026–presentAudio-driven talking headsRIFT-M measurement studyUpdated 13 October 2026

Current status: active exploratory research. The project has narrowed to RIFT-M, a measurement study; the original generative hypothesis remains unvalidated.

Research overview

RIFT investigates predictable motion and residual uncertainty in a frozen talking-head representation. The current RIFT-M study examines the relationship between motion that audio can predict and motion that changes rendered mouth articulation.

The emphasis is on reliable measurement before proposing a generative method. IMTalker provides the upstream representation and renderer.

Evidence to date

An initial predictive pilot did not support the tested formulation. Subsequent calibration work exposed limits in the precision of standard landmark-based measurements, motivating a closer examination of the measurement instrument.

These observations motivate the current study; they do not establish improved generation, useful recoverability, or transfer across identities.

Research contribution and current work

My work includes hypothesis formulation, controlled predictive experiments, failure analysis, and measurement design. Current work qualifies the measurement setup before broader empirical evaluation.

The project remains exploratory. No improved talking-head generation method or submitted paper is claimed.
