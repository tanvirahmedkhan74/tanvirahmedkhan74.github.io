RIFT — Reliability of renderer measurements

When does a small input change cross a hidden discrete choice?

First-author manuscript in preparation

On this page

  • What I found
  • The question
  • How the study works
  • My role
  • Manuscript

What I found so far

The work began with a check that failed. Before using finite differences to describe a talking-head renderer, I tested whether its response was locally linear at the step sizes I planned to use. None of the candidate records passed.

The renderer copies reference features through a hard top-1 selection. I froze those selections at their base choice and left the continuous pathway live. At the first set of states, this removed most of the even response on nearly all of the selected axes. Much of what the probe had measured came from the selection changing, not from the renderer’s local response.

A replication on new states agreed only in part. The same response appeared in most states, but freezing the selection did not remove it completely. The original local-linearity check still fails, and a separate frame-pair study came back ambiguous.

I have not shown that this matters perceptually, that it is common, or that it holds in other renderers. The result is narrower: in this renderer, a finite-difference number cannot be read as a local property until the discrete selections are held fixed.

The question

A finite-difference probe assumes the function it probes is locally smooth. In this renderer a hard feature selection sits inside that path, so a small input step can quietly change which feature is selected. The measured difference then mixes two effects.

How the study works

I separate the two effects with a frozen-index control. The same step is taken once with the selection left free and once with it pinned to its original choice. The gap between the two arms is what the discrete change contributes. The pinned arm is what a local response measurement can legitimately claim.

The scope is one released renderer and its recorded inputs.

My role

This is my own project and I am its first author. I designed the experiments, implemented the probes and the frozen-index intervention, and interpreted the results.

The question came from my renderer work at MILab. I wanted to know what my measurements could reliably tell me before proposing a better generative model.

Manuscript

Working title: A frozen-index audit of finite-difference probes in one released talking-head renderer.

The manuscript is in preparation and has not been submitted. Exact figures stay in the manuscript until it is public.
