RIFT — Reliability of renderer measurements

When does a small input change cross a hidden discrete choice?

First-author manuscript in preparation · Planned target: TMLR

On this page

  • Research question
  • Study design
  • Role & contribution
  • Manuscript & scope

Research question

I study the reliability of finite-difference measurements in a released talking-head renderer. Holding feature selections fixed helps separate changes in discrete choices from responses along continuous pathways.

Study design and measurement

A finite-difference probe assumes the function it probes is locally smooth. In this renderer a hard feature selection sits inside that path, so a small input step can quietly change which feature is selected, and the measured difference then mixes two effects.

The study separates them with a frozen-index control: the same step is taken once with the selection left free and once with it pinned to its original choice. The gap between the two arms is what the discrete change contributes; the pinned arm is what a local response measurement can legitimately claim.

Negative and ambiguous outcomes are kept rather than filtered out. A probe that behaves under one condition and not another is the finding, not a nuisance.

Role and contribution

I lead the experimental design, implementation and interpretation.

I report the conditions under which each probe was taken, so that a measurement is not detached from the configuration that produced it.

Manuscript and scope

Finite-difference probes across hard feature selection: a frozen-index audit of one released talking-head renderer.

First-author manuscript in preparation · Planned target: TMLR.

Author list: anonymous.

The scope is one released renderer. The work does not establish a perceptual remedy or a general result across rendering models.
