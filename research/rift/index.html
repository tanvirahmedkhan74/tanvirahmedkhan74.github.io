RIFT — Recoverability-Gated Implicit Facial Dynamics

How can we study facial motion reliably through a neural renderer?

September 2026–presentAudio-driven facial motion and neural renderingUpdated 28 October 2026

Active research · Methodology and manuscript in development · Not submitted. The research direction continues to evolve.

Facial motion and reliable measurement

Speech constrains facial motion without determining every expression or movement. RIFT began by asking how to model the predictable and uncertain parts of that motion. Studying this question also requires reliable measurements of how a facial representation changes the rendered image.

Work on the original hypothesis led to a closer investigation of the renderer itself. The current research includes controlled studies of measurement reliability and failure cases in a released talking-head system.

Research development

I use experimental findings to revise the assumptions behind the proposed methodology and decide which questions the evidence can support. The renderer investigation has become a research direction in its own right, while the broader facial-motion methodology remains under development.

The present work supports a focused diagnostic study. Improved motion generation, perceptual quality, and transfer across systems remain open questions.

My contribution and current status

My work includes hypothesis formulation, experimental design and implementation, renderer analysis, and interpretation of negative and conditional results. The study builds on an existing representation and renderer.

Research, methodology development, and manuscript preparation are ongoing. Detailed methods, experimental results, and follow-up plans remain private while the direction is being refined.
