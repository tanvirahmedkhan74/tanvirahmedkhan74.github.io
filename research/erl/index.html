ERL — Emergent Retrieval Landmark

Where is useful evidence lost between retrieval and a final answer?

2026–presentRetrieval and multi-hop question answeringUpdated 28 October 2026

Active research · Manuscript in preparation · Not submitted. Experiments, analysis, and manuscript development are ongoing.

The research question

Multi-hop question answering requires a system to find and combine several pieces of evidence. A wrong answer can reflect different problems: necessary information may never be retrieved, useful passages may be discarded, or the reader may fail to use the context it receives. ERL studies how to distinguish these failures.

The project grew out of questions about memory retrieval in PNEUMA. It began with graph-based access to evidence and has developed into a controlled failure analysis of retrieval, evidence selection, and reading.

Research approach

I design comparisons that examine the stages separately and then check how they interact. The central question is when a change that improves evidence availability or selection also improves the final answer, and how that conclusion depends on the baseline and reader.

The work emphasizes controlled evaluation and careful interpretation of negative and conditional findings. The current study does not establish an end-to-end method improvement; further research and analysis remain in progress.

My contribution and current status

My work covers research-question formulation, experimental design, implementation, and analysis, building on established datasets, retrieval methods, and pretrained models.

ERL is active research with a manuscript in preparation. Detailed methods and unpublished results are being kept private while the work continues.
