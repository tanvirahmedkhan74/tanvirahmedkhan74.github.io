ERL — Evidence selection in multi-hop question answering

Where does supporting evidence disappear before a question is answered?

First-author manuscript in preparation · Planned target: ACL

On this page

  • Research question
  • Study design
  • Role & contribution
  • Manuscript & scope

Research question

I trace evidence loss across candidate access, selection and reading in multi-hop question answering. Controlled comparisons distinguish improvements in evidence selection from the conditions under which a reader can use that evidence.

Study design and measurement

The study is organised as a conditional audit rather than a single end-to-end score. Support is followed through three stages — whether a supporting document is retrieved at all, whether it survives selection, and whether a reader that receives it actually uses it — so that a loss at one stage is not read as a failure of another.

Selection results are compared against stronger selection baselines rather than a weak default, because a gain measured against a poor baseline says little about the mechanism. Reader conditions are varied separately, since a selection improvement is only meaningful for a reader able to use the evidence it supplies.

An early routing hypothesis did not hold. That negative result is kept in the manuscript with the conditions under which it was tested, rather than set aside in favour of the comparisons that did work.

Role and contribution

I lead the research design, implementation and analysis.

I distinguish the availability of supporting documents from their selection and from their use in an answer, and I report selection gains together with the reader conditions that qualify them.

Manuscript and scope

From Transit Routing to Evidence Selection: Conditional Audits of Multi-Hop QA Failures.

First-author manuscript in preparation · Planned target: ACL.

Author list: anonymous.

The study does not establish a general end-to-end answer gain.
