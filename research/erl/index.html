Ongoing independent research

ERL — Emergent Retrieval Landmark

Studying where multi-hop question answering loses useful evidence: graph routing, evidence selection, and the reader.

2026–presentMulti-hop question answeringManuscript in preparation

Current status: ongoing evaluation and manuscript revision. The initial routing experiment did not establish an improvement over BM25.

Research question

When does better evidence access improve answers, and which gains survive a change of selector, representation, or reader?

ERL began with the hypothesis that intermediate memories could gain persistent value by routing retrieval toward other evidence. Learned transit routing and BM25 tied on the held-out LoCoMo test under the same inspection budget. That result motivated a controlled failure analysis across LoCoMo, MuSiQue, and 2WikiMultihopQA.

The study separates candidate discovery, evidence selection, and reading. Learned selectors improve support recall in some fixed candidate pools, but comparison questions regress and downstream answer gains depend on the reader checkpoint.

Evidence

Selected findings and their limits

Results are specific to the tested datasets, candidate pools, and reader checkpoints.

StageObserved resultResearch decision
Matched-budget routingLearned transit and BM25 each reached 0.3671 full-support recall on the held-out LoCoMo test, inspecting 34 items.No demonstrated routing advantage in this setting.
Fixed-pool evidence selectionOn 2Wiki official development data, relational S2 support recall was 0.9459 versus 0.9042 for S1. Both models were trained on a fixed 20,000-question target-data subset.A scoped selection gain: five selected paragraphs from a supplied ten-paragraph pool.
Question-type sensitivityS2 improved recall for four-support/bridge questions by 0.1078, while comparison questions regressed by 0.0278.Retain the regression; investigate dataset and lexical-overlap dependence.
Reader sensitivityS2 minus S1 answer F1 was −0.0039 for SmolLM2-360M-Instruct and +0.0112 for SmolLM2-1.7B-Instruct on 2Wiki.Checkpoint-specific outcomes; no general reader-scaling claim.

Scope of the evidence

Fixed-pool selection gains do not establish end-to-end retrieval improvements or zero-shot transfer. The study also leaves open whether gains survive strong rerankers and mechanism controls. LoCoMo findings require caution because evaluation spans only ten conversation banks.

Contribution

Research and implementation boundary

Research and implementation
Matched-budget retrieval experiments, pointwise and relational evidence selectors, reader-context comparisons, result verification, and claim-to-evidence tracking.
External foundations
LoCoMo, MuSiQue, 2WikiMultihopQA, pretrained encoders and readers, and established retrieval and statistical methods.
Evaluation practice
Prospectively specified protocols, frozen artifacts, independent metric recomputation, and explicit retention of negative and conditional results.
Assistance disclosure
The repository records agent-assisted implementation in part of the reviewer-proofing and E0 remedial process. Scientific direction, protocols, gates, and claim decisions are documented as author-owned; the work is not described here as unaided implementation.

Next research questions: test stronger reranking and reader baselines, examine representation and dataset dependence, and connect discovery, selection, and answer quality on the same questions. The manuscript remains in preparation.
