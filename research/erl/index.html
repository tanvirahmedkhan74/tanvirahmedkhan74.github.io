ERL — Evidence Loss in Multi-Hop QA

Where does useful evidence disappear between retrieval and a final answer?

2026–presentMulti-hop question answeringManuscript in preparationUpdated 14 October 2026

Current status: active research, with further experiments and analysis in progress. The manuscript is in preparation.

Research overview

ERL (Emergent Retrieval Landmark) studies evidence loss across retrieval, selection, and reading. The work began with a graph-routing hypothesis and developed into a controlled failure analysis across conversational memory and multi-hop question-answering benchmarks.

The manuscript in preparation, From Transit Routing to Evidence Selection: A Controlled Multi-Hop QA Failure Analysis, evaluates whether local improvements survive stronger baselines and translate into better final answers.

Preliminary findings and interpretation

The initial routing study did not establish an advantage over a matched retrieval baseline. Selection improvements against simpler comparators were substantially limited by stronger rerankers, while answer-level effects depended on the evaluation setting and reader. The tested closed-corpus pipeline did not show an end-to-end benefit.

The ongoing work develops a bounded diagnostic study: identifying where a proposed repair stops helping, while retaining negative results and distinguishing evidence recall from answer quality.

Research contribution and current work

My work covers the research questions, controlled evaluation design, implementation, and analysis. It builds on established datasets, retrieval methods, pretrained models, and statistical tools; parts of the implementation and verification used agent assistance.

Current work includes further experiments, broader evaluation, and manuscript development. The manuscript is in preparation. Preliminary findings do not establish a general retrieval or question-answering improvement.
