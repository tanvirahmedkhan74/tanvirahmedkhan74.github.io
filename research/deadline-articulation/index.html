Deadline articulation — Information available at playback

What can an avatar know when speech becomes playable, and what does waiting add?

First-author manuscript in preparation

On this page

  • What I found
  • The question
  • How the study works
  • My role
  • Manuscript

What I found so far

Full-duplex speech models generate slightly ahead of playback, so their internal states look like free lookahead for lip motion. I found that most of this lookahead comes from the decoding schedule. The step that completes an audio frame also emits the text and semantic tokens of the next frame, and it costs no delay.

Once everything available at the playback deadline is counted as the baseline, waiting adds little. Waiting two further steps, 160 ms, reduced lip-aperture prediction error by about a quarter of the margin I had fixed in advance. The gain stayed flat from one to three steps.

At the deadline, the hidden state still carries articulatory information that the model’s own tokens lack.

The practical reading is simple. An avatar driven by speech-model states should use everything available at the playback deadline before it adds latency to wait for future states.

The question

Natural lip movement leads the sound it belongs to, so a face model benefits from knowing about speech that has not been played yet. Whether a speech model really offers that lookahead depends on an accounting question: what has the model already produced when an audio frame can first be played, and what does waiting for more add?

How the study works

I define a structural baseline: everything the model has produced when a frame becomes playable. The lookahead gain is the drop in lip-prediction error when the predictor may also use later steps. Every information set is decoded by the same linear probe, cross-fitted over conversations with different participants, so a difference comes from the moment the state is read and not from a different model or input.

The margin, the decision rule and the estimator were fixed before any model feature was computed. I also had to correct audio–video offsets in the recordings before the lip measurements could be trusted.

Trained readouts and timing schedules under explicit playback constraints are planned and not done.

My role

This is my own project and I am its first author. I designed the study and implemented it.

The question came from the speech-to-motion bridges I built at MILab, where every extra step of waiting was a delay the listener could feel.

Manuscript

Working title: Deadline-Available Speech States for Full-Duplex Avatar Articulation.

The manuscript is in preparation and has not been submitted. It is not a finished zero-delay avatar method. Exact figures stay in the manuscript until it is public.
